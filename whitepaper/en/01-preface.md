# Preface: From GenAI to Agentic AI — A Paradigm Shift in the Data Foundation

Over the past three years, Generative AI (GenAI) has reshaped the technology and industrial landscape at an unprecedented pace. From the arrival of ChatGPT to the breakthroughs of large models across text, image, audio, and video generation, machines have, for the first time, acquired a "human-brain-like" capacity for understanding and creation. Enterprises broadly recognize that intelligent transformation centered on large models is an unavoidable theme of this generation of technological change.

Since the second half of 2025, however, the industry's focus has been undergoing an even more profound shift: **from a GenAI that "chats and generates" toward an Agentic AI that "plans, calls tools, and autonomously completes tasks."** If GenAI gave AI a "brain," then agents give AI "hands and feet" — and memory. An agent no longer merely responds; it actively perceives its environment, decomposes goals, invokes external systems, makes decisions in sequence, and embeds itself into enterprise business processes to actually *get work done*.

The market signals behind this shift are unmistakable. Gartner predicts that by the end of 2026, **40%** of enterprise applications will embed task-specific AI agents — up from less than 5% in 2025. Among enterprise applications shipped or updated in Q1 2026, **80%** already embed at least one AI agent. The global AI agent market is expected to reach the tens-of-billions-of-dollars range in 2026 and to keep expanding at a CAGR above 40%. The AI agent is becoming the next foundational force — after cloud, mobile, and large models — to reshape the very form of enterprise software.

But the flip side of the hype is a sobering reality. Gartner also warns that by 2027, **over 40%** of agentic AI projects will be canceled — not because models are insufficiently capable, but because of runaway costs, unclear business value, ungovernable risk, and, most critically, **data that isn't ready.** As the capabilities of general-purpose large models become commoditized and "democratized," the deciding factor for whether an agent can deliver stable, trustworthy, and controllable value in real enterprise scenarios has shifted from the *model layer* to the *data layer*.

This is precisely the conviction Matrix Origin has long held. Within the GenAI technology stack, the industry has explored the compute, model, and application layers in depth — yet the **data layer** has been consistently undervalued. The agentic era amplifies this contradiction to the extreme: what an agent needs is not a one-time training corpus, but a **real-time, trustworthy, traceable, multimodal supply of context — accompanied by long-term memory.** Without such a data foundation, even the most powerful model is merely a "forgetful, error-prone genius": it cannot remember its last decision, cannot distinguish fresh from stale or true from false, cannot trace the cause of an error, and certainly cannot meet an enterprise's stringent demands for compliance and audit.
